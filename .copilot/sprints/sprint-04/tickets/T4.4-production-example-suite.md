### Example Problem Design

**Example 1 - Trivial Deterministic**:
- **System**: 1 hydro, 1 thermal, 1 bus (both connected), 2 stages
- **Resources**:
  - Hydro: Storage 50 MWh, turbining 30 MW, productivity 1.0
  - Thermal: Cost $80/MWh, capacity 40 MW
  - Demand: 50 MW per stage (constant)
  - Inflow: 20 MWh per stage (deterministic, zero variance)
- **Balance**: Inflow insufficient alone (40 MWh < 100 MWh demand), requires thermal mix
- **Learning**: Trade-off between using hydro now vs saving for future stage
- **Expected Policy**: ~60% hydro, ~40% thermal (minimize cost while managing storage)
- Purpose: Verify installation, understand JSON format, see basic SDDP mechanics

**Example 2 - Basic Stochastic Hydrothermal**:
- **System**: 2 hydro, 2 thermal, 1 bus (all connected), 2 stages, 5 branchings
- **Resources**:
  - Hydro 1: Storage 80 MWh, turbining 40 MW
  - Hydro 2: Storage 60 MWh, turbining 35 MW
  - Thermal 1: Cost $70/MWh, capacity 50 MW
  - Thermal 2: Cost $90/MWh, capacity 45 MW
  - Demand: 80 MW per stage
  - Inflow: Stochastic (mean 30 MWh, std dev 10 MWh per hydro)
- **Balance**: Expected inflow ~60 MWh vs 80 MWh demand, requires thermal in most scenarios
- **Learning**: Handle uncertainty, manage storage risk across scenarios
- **Expected Policy**: Use hydro aggressively in high-inflow scenarios, conserve in low-inflow
- Purpose: Introduce stochasticity, scenario tree, risk-averse decision making

**Example 3 - Multi-Stage Hydrothermal**:
- **System**: 5 hydro (3 on bus 1, 2 on bus 2), 5 thermal (2 on bus 1, 3 on bus 2), 1 line, 24 stages (monthly)
- **Resources**:
  - Bus 1: 150 MW hydro capacity, 100 MW thermal ($60-$80/MWh)
  - Bus 2: 100 MW hydro capacity, 120 MW thermal ($65-$95/MWh)
  - Line: 80 MW capacity, $5/MWh exchange penalty
  - Demand: Seasonal (100-180 MW, summer high 180 MW, winter low 100 MW)
  - Inflow: Seasonal (wet season Nov-Mar high, dry season Apr-Oct low)
- **Balance**: Total capacity sufficient but requires planning across wet/dry seasons
- **Seasonality**:
  - Wet season (Nov-Mar): High inflow, moderate demand → store water
  - Dry season (Apr-Oct): Low inflow, moderate demand → use stored water + thermal
  - Summer peaks (Dec-Feb): High demand, medium-high inflow → use all resources
- **Learning**: Long-term storage planning, seasonal patterns, network flow
- **Expected Policy**: Store water during wet season, deplete during dry season, trade power between buses
- Purpose: Realistic problem size, convergence analysis, seasonal planning

**Example 4 - Hydrothermal Cascade**:
- **System**: 5 hydro in cascade (upstream→downstream), 5 thermal, 2 buses, 1 line, 24 stages (monthly), 20 branchings
- **Resources**:
  - Hydro cascade: Total 150 MW, storages 100→70→50→40→30 MWh (upstream to downstream)
    - Upstream receives natural inflow only
    - Each downstream receives spillage + turbining from upstream
  - Thermals: Distributed, total 120 MW, costs $60-$95/MWh
  - Demand: Seasonal (110-190 MW monthly variation)
  - Inflow: Seasonal + stochastic (20 branchings capture more uncertainty)
- **Balance**: Cascade creates storage coupling, spillage penalties (~$1/MWh) discourage waste
- **Cascade Dynamics**:
  - Upstream decisions affect all downstream reservoirs
  - Spillage from upstream adds to downstream storage
  - Must balance: local optimization vs system-wide coordination
- **Learning**: Coupled reservoir decisions, spillage management, cascade flow optimization
- **Expected Policy**: Coordinate releases to minimize total cost + spillage, use upstream storage strategically
- Purpose: Hydro cascade constraints, downstream dependencies, coupled state variables

**Example 5 - Large-Scale Brazilian System**:
- **System**: ~160 hydros (multiple cascades), ~120 thermals, 5 buses (interconnected), 60 stages (5 years monthly), 20 branchings
- **Resources**:
  - Hydro: ~60,000 MW total capacity (Brazil-like hydropower dominance)
    - Major cascades: Paraná basin (7 reservoirs), Amazon basin (5 reservoirs), others
    - Storage capacity: 50,000-100,000 MWh system-wide
  - Thermal: ~40,000 MW total capacity
    - Nuclear: ~$40/MWh (base load, 3,000 MW)
    - Coal: ~$60/MWh (medium, 8,000 MW)
    - Natural gas: ~$80-$120/MWh (flex, 20,000 MW)
    - Oil/diesel: ~$150/MWh (peak, 9,000 MW)
  - Network: 5 buses with 2,000-5,000 MW transmission lines
    - North bus: High hydro concentration
    - Southeast bus: High demand center
    - South bus: Balanced mix
    - Northeast bus: Thermal-heavy, limited hydro
    - Central-West bus: Medium size
  - Demand: 50,000-85,000 MW system-wide (seasonal + regional)
    - Summer (Dec-Feb): Peak 85,000 MW (air conditioning load)
    - Winter (Jun-Aug): ~55,000 MW (lower but dry season)
    - Annual average: ~68,000 MW
  - Inflow: Highly seasonal and regional
    - Wet season (Nov-Mar): 3-5× dry season inflow
    - Dry season (Apr-Oct): Minimum inflows, rely on storage
    - Regional variation: North has different pattern than South
- **Balance**: System designed to match Brazilian dispatch challenge
  - Wet season: Abundant hydro, minimal thermal (low cost)
  - Dry season: Depleted reservoirs, heavy thermal usage (high cost)
  - Critical period: End of dry season with low storage
- **Seasonality Pattern** (5-year horizon):
  - Year 1-5: Repeating seasonal pattern with stochastic variation
  - Algorithm must learn multi-year planning (save water for critical periods)
- **Learning**: Large-scale coordination, multi-region optimization, long-term planning (60 months)
- **Expected Policy**: 
  - Wet season: 90% hydro, 10% thermal base load
  - Dry season: 60% hydro (stored), 40% thermal
  - Critical periods: 40% hydro, 60% thermal (use expensive peakers if needed)
  - Network: Transfer excess hydro power from North/South to Southeast demand center
- Purpose: Performance benchmark, scalability test, production-quality problem, realistic dispatch challenge
- Runtime: <5 minutes (demonstrates production performance)

### Creating Problem JSON Files

Use existing `example/` directory as template structure:

**Required Files**:
- `config.json`: SDDP algorithm configuration (convergence, iterations, threads)
- `system.json`: Physical system (generators, reservoirs, buses, lines, costs, capacities)
- `graph.json`: Scenario tree structure (stages, branchings, probabilities)
- `recourse.json`: Stochastic data (inflow distributions, demand patterns)

**Key Principles for Resource Balancing**:
1. **Avoid Trivial Solutions**:
   - Don't make demand >> capacity (all deficit, no learning)
   - Don't make capacity >> demand (zero cost, no learning)
   - Don't make inflow >> demand (always cheap hydro, no thermal trade-off)

2. **Enable Meaningful Optimization**:
   - Hydro capacity < demand (requires thermal sometimes)
   - Thermal capacity > demand (always feasible, but costly)
   - Storage < total demand over horizon (forces rationing decisions)
   - Inflow seasonal (wet/dry creates storage planning challenge)

3. **Create Trade-offs**:
   - Use hydro now (cheap) vs save for later (risk)
   - Use expensive thermal now vs deficit later
   - Store water in wet season vs spill (cascade coordination)
   - Transfer power between buses vs use local thermal
